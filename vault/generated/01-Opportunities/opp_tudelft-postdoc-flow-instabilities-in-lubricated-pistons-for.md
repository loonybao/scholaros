## Description

Challenge: To compress hydrogen efficiently.
Change: Develop a new design to achieve unmatched efficiencies.
Impact: Contribute to the backbone of the hydrogen economy.
Job description
In the transition towards a green economy, hydrogen is called to play a pivotal role. However, a major roadblock is its storage and later distribution and dispensing. To this aim, the
HyMEGA project aims at developing a new compressor design that achieves higher performance within a consortia of industrial and academic partners. In this particular part of
the project, we want to assess the two-phase flow dynamics of a lubricated piston, integrating, kinematic, fluid mechanics and heat transfer with different levels of fidelity.
You will work with Dr. Nicolas Valle within the faculty of Mechanical Engineering.
Your tasks will be to develop mathematical models that assist all the design stages: from dimensioning to the selection of lubricants to the control strategies; in close cooperation with the partners of the consortium.Your first task will be to develop and implement a low order model to predict relevant outcomes (performance, gas cleanliness, capacity, etc) of the compressor. This first model will guide an initial design and control strategies of a first prototype. Subsequently, you will develop a comprehensive, high fidelity CFD model that enables additional level on insight into the physics and thus refines the development of the design, with particular emphasis on two-phase flow phenomena like separation, coalescence and heat transfer effects.
Job requirements
PhD in Mechanical/Aerospace/Maritime/Chemical Engineering, Physics, Applied Mathematics, or a related discipline.
Affinity for collaborative research between academic and industrial stakeholders.
Proven* track record in multiphase fluid mechanics.
Proven* modelling experience.
Proven* CFD experience.
In addition, the ideal candidate should have:
Experience* with commercial or academic software (OpenFOAM, Fluent, Basilisk) is a plus.
Experience* in VoF, Level Set, Phase Field or similar methods is a plus.
Experience* in moving mesh computations is a plus.
