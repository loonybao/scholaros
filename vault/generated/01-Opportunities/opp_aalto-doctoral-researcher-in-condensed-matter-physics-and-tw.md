Gate-tunable 2D devices are at the cutting edge of condensed matter physics today. This is where many of the most exciting discoveries are being made, and the field is moving fast. You will work at the frontier of this research, combining nanofabricated devices made from 2D layered materials with low-temperature scanning tunneling microscopy (STM). Our group has long experience with both the techniques and the materials.
Your role and goals
Your goal is to study emergent electronic phenomena in gate-tunable van der Waals materials using STM. A gate voltage lets you add or remove electrons in a device, so you can tune one sample through many different quantum phases, including exotic ones that are hard to reach in any other way. With STM, you can see these phases at the atomic scale and learn how they form and behave.
Your tasks will include:
Fabrication of quantum devices in cleanroom facilities, including lithography, deposition, exfoliation and transfer of 2D materials
Running low-temperature STM experiments on these devices
Collaboration with leading theoretical physicists
Analysis of measured data and writing scientific articles

What we offer
We offer a fully funded PhD project for one talented Doctoral Researcher under the guidance of Dr. Viliam Vaňo in the Atomic Scale Physics group led by Prof. Peter Liljeroth. The group consists currently of 3 Academy Research Fellows, 1 Postdoctoral Researcher and 6 Doctoral Researchers. The group focuses studying physical phenomena in atomically precise materials characterized using scanning tunneling microscopy in a wide array of systems such as heterostructures of two-dimensional van der Waals materials, atomically well-defined graphene nanoribbons, and artificial lattices on surfaces.
