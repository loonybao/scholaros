## Description

PhD position in Operations management for E-commerce logistics: leading the transition to reusable packages
Job description
E-commerce has become a strong component of our daily lives. The rise of webshops and platform technology gives us the opportunity to have the desired products at home in no time. However, the ease of this process, its increased adoption during COVID times, along with policies to guarantee consumer satisfaction, has been generating a massive volume of parcels around the globe and, very frequently, consequent returns of products to the seller. Although the logistics service providers around these transport flows have reacted with heavy process optimization, the waste generated is substantial and inevitable when the loading unit is a carton box (CB).
The use of CBs has several financial and environmental drawbacks. First, CBs cannot be reutilized, and because of this, they are continuously produced and not entirely made with recycled material.  Secondly, from a logistical standpoint, the rigid composition of these boxes, which cannot always perfectly fit the product inside, inevitably causes the shipping of air. In turn, this causes an inefficient utilization of the capacity of the means of transport. Thirdly, the waste management of papers affects municipalities due to the extra handling costs, sometimes increased by wrong recycling behaviors. Consumers' satisfaction, experience, and environmental perception are not fully positive when dealing with a single-use box that needs to be torn and thrown at the end of the life cycle. In response to this, the European Commission has only recently launched an action plan to limit these drawbacks, a sign that while the problem is acknowledged, we are still far from globally implementable solutions.
The development and the adoption of flexible, durable, reusable, and smart packages, which we define RELABELs, can solve the aforementioned problems generated by single-use carton boxes. Digital features can favour tracking and autonomous decisions, which depend, for example, on the recipient's availability, in line with the Physical Internet guidelines. For example, a RELABEL could autonomously suggest a slower mode of transport if the recipient is unavailable in a close time window, relieving the pressure on the transport system. RELABELs can substantially reduce the CO2 footprint, improve customer satisfaction, generate better value for transport capacity, and improve the efficiency of packing procedures. Their reusable nature has the potential to reduce packaging expenditures, while their adaptable dimensions can contribute to lowering transportation costs. Furthermore, environmental benefits may be realized through the reduction of packaging waste and decreased transportation-related emissions.
Within the NWO-funded project, named in short "Relabel it", the PhD candidate is expected to research several logistics challenges ineherent to a full adoption of the reusable packages.
There are several aspects that require attention to exploit the features of flexible packages.
1. Packing Model: The model will be developed for LSPs and used for packing RELABELs into transport units, e.g., trucks, whilst minimizing the required size of the fleet that carries out transportation.
2. Storage Location Model: The model will be used to determine the number and the geographical locations of RELABELs' return and pickup points in a city.
3. RELABELs Collection Model: We study how RELABELs are collected and repositioned to their storage locations, and the frequency of the collection to make sure that empties are readily available. Moreover, it is relevant to investigate how the LSP could integrate the return of empties into its schedule.
4. Return Mechanism Design Model: We aim to study the mechanisms that can incentivize swift returns; we will learn lessons from the sea container shipping industry and current deposit system in the food industry
5. Manufacturing, filling and refurbishing: several challenges related to production, the filling process and refurbishment can be expected
