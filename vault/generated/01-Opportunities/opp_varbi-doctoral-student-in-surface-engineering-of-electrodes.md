Project description
Third-cycle subject:
Chemical Science and Engineering
Flow batteries are an excellent technology for stationary and long-duration (8+ hours) energy storage. In terms of cost and output power, the electrode is a critical component that should meet requirements for high electrical conductivity, large surface area, electrocatalytic activity, and chemo-mechanical stability. More research is needed to understand the relationship between surface properties and output power to advance electrode development. This project aims to gain fundamental and engineering insights into the electrodes in flow batteries through combining novel electrode surface treatment using 2D transition-metal carbides at KTH with carbon nanofiber development activities at Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU). The experimental results will be used to develop a multiphysics model to calculate electrode properties and predict electrode utilization, current density distribution, and power density limits. The PhD candidate will spend a year at China-UK Low Carbon College, SJTU. We are searching for a person with interest in electrochemistry and batteries combined with a foundation in material characterization.
