The Aerosol and Cloud Particle Optics group, part of the Aerosol Physics Laboratory at Tampere University, is recruiting a Doctoral Researcher to join CRYSTAL, a European Research Council (ERC)-funded project that pioneers the use of advanced 3D nano-printing in atmospheric science. CRYSTAL addresses a major unresolved question: how nano- and microscale surface roughness changes the way atmospheric ice crystals scatter sunlight and how cirrus clouds are represented in climate and satellite-observation models.
Job description
As a doctoral researcher, you will investigate the optical response of realistic ice crystal surface roughness using nano-printed test surfaces. Your main responsibility will be to plan and participate in the optical characterization of these surfaces and to analyse polarization-resolved measurements of their bidirectional reflectance distribution function (BRDF). The work combines optics, precision measurement, surface characterization, programming and quantitative data analysis.
You will contribute to selecting and preparing representative ice crystal surface morphologies for nano-printing and to assessing the quality of the fabricated samples. You will develop robust data-processing and quality-control routines and determine how the measured scattering depends on roughness scale, surface anisotropy, illumination and observation geometry, and polarization.
In collaboration with the project's postdoctoral researcher, you will test physically based surface-scattering descriptions, including Harvey-Shack theory and ABg-type parameterizations, against the laboratory measurements. The resulting parameterized optical description will support modelling of atmospheric ice crystals and, ultimately, improved interpretation of cirrus-cloud observations from next-generation satellites.
Requirements
Applicants must hold an internationally recognized Master's degree in experimental physics, optics, photonics, atmospheric science or another relevant field by the starting date. You should be motivated by experimental research and interested in working across the boundaries of atmospheric science, optical measurement and surface technology.
The most important qualifications are hands-on experience with optical or other precision laboratory measurements and strong programming and data-analysis skills in Python, MATLAB or comparable tools. Previous experience with light-scattering or BRDF measurements, gonioreflectometry, surface metrology, microscopy, nano-fabrication, ice-particle optics or cloud microphysics is an advantage but not required. Excellent English and constructive teamwork are essential; Finnish is not required.
