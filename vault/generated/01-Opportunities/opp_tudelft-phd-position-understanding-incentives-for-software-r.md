## Description

Why do teams build new software rather than reuse existing solutions? Investigate how technology, incentives and organisational behaviour shape these choices in this PhD project.
Job description
Software reuse promises to reduce duplicated effort and help organisations develop technology at scale. Teams build data assets, software components, APIs and AI agents that other teams can adopt. Yet making these resources available does not necessarily mean they will be reused. What appears efficient for the organisation as a whole may create additional costs, dependencies or risks for individual teams.
This raises a research question that extends across organisations: under what conditions do teams choose to reuse existing solutions, and when do they build their own? Technical explanations, such as integration difficulties or poor documentation, may tell only part of the story. Performance targets, budget allocation, professional norms and regulatory requirements may also shape these decisions. Understanding how these factors interact is essential to explaining when reuse succeeds—and when developing a separate solution makes sense.
This PhD project investigates these questions through empirical research within a large international bank. A particular focus is how people become attached to their own solutions and their ideas of what “good” looks like—and how this shapes their willingness to adopt alternatives. You will use evidence from this setting to develop and test explanations relevant to the wider challenge of organising software reuse.
As a PhD researcher, you will conduct interdisciplinary research at the intersection of software engineering, economics and organisational behaviour. Your work will include:
Interviewing engineers, data scientists, product owners, architects and managers to understand how teams decide whether to build or reuse.
Analysing internal usage data to measure reuse and identify differences across teams, business units and types of software assets.
Examining how performance targets, ownership arrangements, budgets and regulatory requirements influence these decisions.
Comparing teams’ stated reasons with their observed behaviour to understand which explanations are supported by the evidence.
Developing proposals to improve reuse and evaluating selected interventions in practice.
The project draws on theories of incentives, transaction costs and the governance of shared resources. You will test existing assumptions and develop new explanations, including findings that challenge prevailing ideas about what enables effective reuse.
The research will be conducted in close collaboration with the partner bank. You will regularly spend time within the organisation and work with its experts. This collaboration provides an empirical setting for studying how technical and organisational factors interact across teams, business units and jurisdictions.
Your four-year PhD is part of a joint research programme between TU Delft and the partner bank. The programme brings together four PhD projects investigating incentives for reuse, resource orchestration, the evolution of software assets and reliable AI-assisted software development. Your findings will inform related work on improving support for reuse.
You will be supervised by Professor Michel van Eeten and Dr Yury Zhauniarovich at TU Delft’s Faculty of Technology, Policy and Management. Your project will be embedded in an interdisciplinary team of more than 20 researchers, bringing together computer science, social science and other disciplines. PhD researchers collaborate on research and publications, supported by daily scrum sessions, a weekly colloquium and monthly sprint planning. These activities sustain a culture of collaborative research and innovation.
