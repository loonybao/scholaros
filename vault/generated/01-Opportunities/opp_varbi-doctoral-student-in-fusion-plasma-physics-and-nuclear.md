Project description
Third-cycle subject:
Electrical Engineering
The provision of plasma-facing components (PFCs) with sufficient lifetime is one of the major technological obstacles to be overcome in developing fusion reactors. Recently, the damage of PFCs due to interaction with runaway electrons (RE) has become a highly critical issue in evaluations of disruption consequences.
ITER
, currently seeks for the validation of relevant numerical models and tools against existing experimental evidence in order to gain confidence in their applications to predictive reactor modelling.
PhD project will focus on the development of modelling tools for simulations of RE-induced PFC damage and their validation against data from
EUROfusion Consortium
, and
DIII-D (USA)
. The validated models will be employed for predictive modelling of specific ITER scenarios following input from the ITER Organization. The modelling of MeV RE energy deposition in PFCs will be performed with Monte Carlo simulations. These results will serve as input for the thermomechanical models to be developed in FEM and CFD software to address severe deformation and potential explosion of the material.
We offer expertise in the theory and modelling of plasma-surface interactions and electron transport into matter, access to state-of-the-art experimental data from the largest tokamaks worldwide and close collaboration with the Experiments & Plasma Operation Section of the ITER Organization.
