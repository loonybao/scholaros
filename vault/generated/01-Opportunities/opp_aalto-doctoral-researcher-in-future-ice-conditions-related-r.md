The School of Engineering is inviting applications for a Doctoral Researcher in future ice conditions related risks on offshore wind and related energy systems
Research background and duties
At the Department of Energy and Mechanical Engineering, our Marine and Arctic Technology group develops numerical models of sea ice, such as discrete element simulations, to study ice loads on offshore structures and ship operations in ice. In this role, you will contribute to the ICROSS project, funded by the Det Norske Veritas Foundation, by studying how offshore wind farms and a changing climate alter sea ice conditions, such as ice deformation, ridging and fast ice. You will assess what these changes mean for ice loading and related risks to offshore wind infrastructure, such as damage to seabed cables from ridge keels, and for the safety of winter navigation in and near wind farms. Your results may also influence future design standards. The research is based on high-resolution numerical modelling of sea ice and may include coupling with large-scale sea ice models and analysis of observational data. The precise focus will be shaped together with your supervisor, the project team and stakeholders. Depending on the focus, your work may include:
Studying existing analyses, models and data on sea ice dynamics, ridging and ice–structure interaction
Further development of discrete element (DEM) simulations or continuum models for sea ice; DEM possibly coupled with large-scale sea ice models
Estimating how wind farms and climate change alter ice conditions and the resulting risks to offshore wind infrastructure
Providing ice condition results for assessing winter navigation risks induced by offshore wind farms
Contributing to the project's integrated cascading risk framework for offshore wind energy and shipping
Publishing in leading journals, presenting at conferences, joining stakeholder workshops and assisting in advising master's students
You will work closely with the ICROSS project team and with collaborators at CSC – IT Center for Science and the Finnish Meteorological Institute. Your doctoral research will be supervised by Associate Professor Arttu Polojärvi, and you will be part of the Marine and Arctic Technology research group. This is an exciting time to join us: offshore wind is expanding rapidly into ice-covered Nordic seas, and you will work with state-of-the-art sea ice simulation tools on questions that matter to industry and authorities.
