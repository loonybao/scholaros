Project description
Third-cycle subject: Electrical Engineering
Funded by FFI, the TERMINATOR project investigates how fast-switching silicon carbide (SiC) and gallium nitride (GaN) power electronics affect the long-term reliability of electrical motors. Fast voltage transitions (high dv/dt of 20-50 kV/µs) create electro-thermal stresses that speed up stator insulation dielectric aging and damage bearings. Systematic experimental data linking power electronic switching to material degradation is lacking.
As a doctoral student, you will work at the cutting edge of electrification. Your main tasks will include:
Developing automated laboratory platforms for controlled electro-thermal aging experiments.
Conducting accelerated aging tests on stator insulation and bearings under controlled laboratory conditions.
Analyzing experimental data to develop predictive lifetime models using statistics, signal processing, and machine learning.
Validating degradation models on laboratory motor test benches under inverter excitation.
You will join the Electrical Machines and Drives group at KTH, a dynamic research environment, working closely with industrial partners: Traton AB, ABB AB, and ABB Robotics Sweden AB. You will report to Associate Professor Luca Peretti.
