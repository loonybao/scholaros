## Description

Help shape the next generation of aircraft. At TU Delft, you will develop advanced simulation and optimization tools to improve engine-airframe integration, aerodynamic efficiency and noise performance.
Job description
The next generation of aircraft calls for innovative propulsion technologies, more efficient airframes and improved noise performance. But how can we accurately predict the complex interactions between engines and airframes? As a postdoctoral researcher at TU Delft, you will develop a multi-fidelity computational framework to optimize aircraft engine integration, aerodynamic performance and aeroacoustics. Your research will contribute to the design and assessment of future aircraft.
You will work on
MENTOR (Multi-fidelity Engine Integration Optimization for Next Generation Aircraft)
, a research project bringing together experts from TU Delft and the Royal Netherlands Aerospace Centre (NLR). Your challenge is to develop an integrated toolbox combining high-fidelity simulations, fast aerodynamic prediction methods and aeroacoustic models.
Your research will focus on three interconnected areas:
High-fidelity aerodynamic optimization:
Develop a design tool combining adjoint-based optimization with unsteady Reynolds-Averaged Navier–Stokes (URANS) simulations to integrate different propulsion systems with low-drag airframes.
Fast performance prediction:
Combine blade element momentum (BEM) theory and vortex lattice methods to efficiently predict propulsive efficiency and engine-airframe drag.
Aeroacoustic prediction:
Develop models compatible with NLR tools, using flow distortion theory to predict tonal noise and stochastic optimization approaches for broadband noise components.
MENTOR is part of the
Dutch Aviation Systems Analysis Lab
(DASAL)
, a flagship collaboration between TU Delft and NLR, funded through the Dutch National Growth Fund's
Luchtvaart in Transitie
programme. DASAL develops integrated models and simulations to support innovation in the Dutch aviation sector and inform public policy.
You will join the
Faculty of Aerospace Engineering
at TU Delft the Department of
Flow Physics and Technology
. Here, you will work in an interdisciplinary environment alongside PhD candidates, postdoctoral researchers and academic staff. Collaborating closely with NLR experts and researchers across the faculty, you will connect advanced computational methods with real-world aircraft design challenges and contribute to the development of more efficient and quieter aircraft.
