## Description

Mode II fatigue delamination tests only go in one direction, but what happens if we reverse the shear?
Job description
To build the next generation of advanced composite materials, including for example those with morphing capabilities, we need to go beyond the use of traditional lay-ups such as cross-ply or quasi-isotropic. However, evaluating the fatigue performance of such non-traditional laminates is difficult. Current state-of-the-art methods require extensive testing which has to be repeated for each change of lay-up.
Building on the results of the D-STANDART project, the Duramorph project aims to advance our ability to predict the fatigue behaviour of arbitrary lay-ups, based on minimal testing. Finding out how minimal that testing can be, and which tests remain needed is part of the aim of Duramorph. Within Duramoprh, your position will focus on investigating the effect of fibre orientation on mode II delamination growth in CFRP lay-ups. Of particular interest is investigating the fatigue behaviour under reversed shear (R<0) conditions, for which you will need to develop a new test set-up. Throughout the project you will be collaborating with researchers at the other project partners: NLR, University of Twente and XCTE (a start-up focusing on high frequency testing) in order to algin on the overall project outcomes. You will also be expected to communicate your work to the project's industrial advisory board and to outside stakeholders. In addition you will have the opportunity to participate in educational activities at TU Delft, should you wish to increase your experience in that area.
In this position you will be a part of the Damage Management and Certification team (PI: John-Alan Pascoe), having the opportunity to interact and collaborate with a supportive community of MSc thesis students, PhD candidates and post-docs working on related fracture and fatigue topics. This group is part of the Aerospace Structures and Materials department of the Faculty of Aerospace Engineering, and has access to extensive lab facilities for both manufacturing and testing of aerospace materials, including composites.
In summary, the expected activities in this position are:
Develop a test-rig for conducting mode II fatigue delamination growth experiments.
Investigate the effect of fibre orientation on mode II fatigue delamination growth under both R >0 and R < 0 loading.
Communicate your findings via journal papers and conference presentations.
Present your findings to project partners and the industrial advisory board.
Participate in project management.
Participate in project meetings to ensure alignment with other project partners.
