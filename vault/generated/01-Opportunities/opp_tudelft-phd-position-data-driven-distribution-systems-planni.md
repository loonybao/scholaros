## Description

Job Description
The Delft University of Technology is hiring a doctoral candidate on the subject  "Data-Driven Distribution Systems Planning with District Heating".
The decarbonization of the heating sector in the Netherlands is resulting in an increased adoption of district heating at the city scale. Depending on the type of district heating system and its interface with the electricity infrastructure, uncoordinated operation and poor planning may increase congestion at the medium-voltage level. In this PhD project, we will investigate how the electricity infrastructure can accommodate district heating networks by properly coordinating their future operation. To do this, we first investigate which data are needed (from distribution system operators, municipalities, users, etc.) to properly model future district heating demand and operations. We will also aim to understand what the barriers are to obtaining and using such data. As more data becomes available, we will investigate the role of artificial intelligence and machine learning models in facilitating coordinated planning, as well as how to incorporate risk and uncertainty. This PhD Position is part of the NWO Share the “Warmth Project: Data Sharing for Urban Heat Transition Planning”. The Sharing the Warmth Project aims to help Dutch cities move toward affordable, sustainable, and gas-free heating. The project brings citizens, municipalities, and grid operators together to share data in a safe and trustworthy way. By developing privacy-preserving tools and fair data-sharing arrangements, this project strengthens cooperation and informed decision-making. This enables municipalities to plan heating upgrades more efficiently and creates a fairer, more future-proof energy system for everyone.
This is a four-year doctoral appointment. You will be jointly supervised by Dr. Pedro P. Vergara (Associate Professor), Prof. Peter Palensky (Chair IEPG) and Dr. Ivo Potof (CEG Faculty). You will be a member of the section Intelligent Electrical Power Grids in the Faculty of Electrical Engineering, Mathematics, and Computer Science. The project will offer opportunities to collaborate with industrial partners but also with academics from other disciplines, as required (mathematics, operations research). Within the team, we strive to develop methods that are mathematically rigorous and have near-term application potential. We are strong supporters of open science (publishing, source code, data). You will also be expected to assist in teaching activities (student supervision, labs) related to your subject area.
About the ESE Department
The research in the Department of Electrical Sustainable Energy is inspired by the technical, scientific, and societal challenges originating from the transition towards a more sustainable society and focuses on four areas:
DC Systems, Energy Conversion and Storage (DCE&S).
Photovoltaic Materials and Devices (PVMD).
High Voltage Technologies (HVT).
Intelligent Electrical Power Grids (IEPG).
The Electrical Sustainable Energy Department provides expertise in each of these areas throughout the entire energy system chain. The department owns a large ESP laboratory assembling High Voltage testing, DC Grids testing environment, and large RTDS that is actively used for real-time simulation of future electrical power systems, AC and DC protection and wide-area monitoring and protection.
The Intelligent Electrical Power Grid (IEPG) group, headed by Professor Peter Palensky, works on the future of our power system. The goal is to generate, transmit and use electrical energy in a highly reliable, efficient, stable, clean, affordable, and safe way. IEPG integrates new power technologies and smart controls, which interact with other systems and allow for more distributed and variable generation.
