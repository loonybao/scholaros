The School of Engineering is inviting applications for a Doctoral Researcher in Risk Assessment for Offshore Wind Energy Installations and Winter Navigation Ship Operations.
Research background and duties
At the Department of Energy and Mechanical Engineering, our Marine and Arctic Technology group study the analysis of risks in the dynamic interactions of maritime shipping and the functionality of critical maritime infrastructure in extreme weather conditions. We are seeking a motivated doctoral researcher to join our research group and contribute to an interdisciplinary project on the safety and resilience of winter navigation in the vicinity of offshore wind energy installations (OWEI). The position focuses on risk assessment for coupled offshore wind and winter-navigation systems, addressing how offshore wind farms, changing sea-ice conditions, and increasing maritime traffic jointly affect navigational safety and operational resilience. Offshore wind farms can modify sea-ice dynamics and introduce new hazards for winter navigation. The doctoral researcher will develop and apply methods to quantify these effects and to identify conditions under which vessels may face ice-related risks, reduced manoeuvrability, besetting, or drift towards offshore infrastructure. The work will support safer offshore wind siting, operational planning, and decision-making in complex winter maritime environments.
Your work will include:
Identifying high-risk ice, metocean, and traffic conditions that may lead to vessel besetting, loss of manoeuvrability, or drift towards offshore infrastructure. Link ice deformation, drift, vessel capability, and power demand to determine when icebreaker support, convoy operations, or operational restrictions are needed.
Producing risk indicators and site-specific mitigation recommendations for OWEI planning under future ice conditions.
Develop a risk-informed approach for assessing how offshore wind energy installations may affect winter navigation under future sea-ice conditions. Build on Nordic winter navigation practices and integrate maritime traffic risk, navigation safety, ice conditions, wind-farm layout design, logistics, and cost considerations.
Examining how route changes, traffic conflicts, ice-related constraints, safety buffers, and distance to port influence operational safety and project feasibility.
Delivering a framework for safer offshore wind siting and more realistic decision-making in complex winter maritime environments.
You will work closely with the ICROSS project team and with collaborators at local maritime authorities. Your doctoral research will be supervised by Associate Professor Osiris A. Valdez Banda, and you will be part of the Marine and Arctic Technology research group. This is an exciting time to join us: offshore wind is expanding rapidly into ice-covered Nordic seas, and you will work with state-of-the-art risk assessment tools for coupled OWEI and winter navigation systems that are essential to authorities and other key partners in maritime safety assurance.
