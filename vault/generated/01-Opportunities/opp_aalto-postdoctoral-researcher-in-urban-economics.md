We are now looking for a
Postdoctoral Researcher in Urban Economics (2 years)
Do you want to study how homeownership shapes local politics, neighbourhoods and housing supply, using exceptionally rich register data?
We are now looking for a postdoctoral researcher to join the Research Council of Finland project “Societal Impacts of Homeownership” at the Department of Economics, Aalto University. The position is for two years, starting no later than September 2027. In this position you will have a chance to make an impact by producing research on how homeownership shapes local political behaviour and decision-making, and what this means for neighbourhood amenities and housing supply. We welcome applicants with experience in applied microeconomics broadly defined and prefer candidates with strengths in urban, housing or spatial economics.
The project uses Finnish administrative registers that link residents, local politicians and elections to neighbourhoods and detailed housing outcomes. It is a collaboration between Aalto University and the VATT Institute for Economic Research within the Helsinki GSE urban economics community, which offers an active seminar environment, excellent data infrastructure and travel support. Alongside the project, you will have time and mentorship to develop your own research agenda.
Your role and goals
Carry out empirical research for the project using Finnish administrative register data on residents, local politicians, elections, neighbourhoods and housing outcomes
Contribute to modelling work that informs policy-relevant counterfactuals
Write papers with the project team and present them at seminars and conferences
Pursue your own independent research projects, with dedicated time and support from the project team
Teach one six-week course per academic year on quantitative methods in spatial economics or a related field
Take part in graduate student supervision
Your network and team
The project is led by Tuukka Saarimaa (Aalto University), who will be your supervisor. The project team includes researchers at Aalto University and the VATT Institute for Economic Research.
