Aalto University
Department of Energy and Mechanical Engineering
is a community of around 300 staff members assembled to research, teach, create, and develop solutions for society’s needs. Key research areas include marine and arctic technology; energy technologies and systems; mechanical systems and mechatronics; and materials, manufacturing, and product development. We have strong links to industry, creating lasting impacts in both business and society. The
Energy Conversion and Systems (ECS) Research group
has very strong competences in energy system modelling, optimization, and technologies. ECS research covers various aspects of the energy value chain, from production by sustainable energy sources, to energy conversion, storage and end use, and the group annually produces around 130-150 scientific highly ranked journal articles, 5-10 doctoral theses, and contributes to many national and international research projects.
We are now looking for a doctoral researcher to develop dynamic models for assessing the energy system and societal risks due to climate change impacts on offshore wind energy infrastructure in Arctic and sub-Arctic conditions. In this role, you will contribute to the ICROSS project, funded by the Det Norske Veritas Foundation, by studying climate change-induced hazards to offshore wind power and their impacts on future power system resilience. You will assess how these hazard levels can change with evolving conditions. Your work will contribute to the development of an integrated model for cascading societal risk pathways arising from climate hazards to offshore wind energy and maritime operations.
Your role and goals
The work will include the following.
Studying climate-induced hazards to offshore wind energy, using the outputs of ice condition assessments and projections related to climate change induced variations in environmental conditions in Arctic and sub-Arctic areas
Analysing how the above hazards translate to offshore wind energy infrastructure failures and sub-optimal performance and therefore lead to broader power system risks
Developing a system dynamics model to depict the consequential power system risks under varying scenarios
Conducting conditional probability assessments for failure events and carrying out risk prioritization
Developing cascading societal risk pathways and expanding the system dynamic model for multi-dimensional economic impact assessment
The research will heavily involve mathematical model development and may also involve the use of specialised software for system dynamics, risk assessments, simulations etc. The researcher is also expected to carry out collaborative work with external stakeholders such as industry partners, depending on the project needs. Therefore, the work will also require ongoing stakeholder engagement and communication. The primary workplace is Aalto University’s Otaniemi Campus. The work requires in-person presence on the campus.
The doctoral degree is granted after successfully completing a set of advanced taught courses (30 ECTS), writing a doctoral thesis (usually based on publications), and defending the thesis in a public examination. During the duration of the doctoral studies, the researcher will be expected to present publish in leading academic journals and high-level scientific conferences, actively participate in the research group activities, assist in the preparation of new research proposals, engage in teaching-related duties, and help to advise master’s students during the preparation of their final project.
Your network and team
The doctoral researcher will be a part of the Energy Conversion and Systems research group in the Department of Energy and Mechanical Engineering. The supervisor will be Prof. Hirushie Karunathilake. The selected candidate will work closely with the ICROSS project team and with collaborators at CSC – IT Center for Science and the Finnish Meteorological Institute. This is an exciting time to join us: offshore wind is expanding rapidly into ice-covered Nordic seas. You will work with state-of-the-art risk assessment and dynamic modelling tools on critical issues that are highly relevant to authorities and other key partners, in collaboration with other researchers working on sea ice formation, winter navigation, and maritime safety.
