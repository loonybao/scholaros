## Description

Join an interdisciplinary team and use unique North Sea field observations to uncover how waves, currents, and sediment transport drive the recovery of offshore sand extraction pits.
Job description
The BACI Sand Extraction project is a multidisciplinary research programme that aims to understand the ecological, hydrodynamic, and morphodynamic impacts of offshore sand extraction. Using a Before-After-Control-Impact (BACI) approach, the project aims to unravel the processes driving the physical and ecological recovery of a medium-depth sand extraction pit over timescales ranging from individual storms to multiple years.
As part of the project, an intensive field experiment is currently taking place at a newly created 6 m deep sand extraction pit in the Dutch North Sea, and will be repeated at a later stage. Instrumented measurement frames have been installed in and around the extraction pit to monitor waves, turbulence levels, current profiles, suspended sediment concentrations, and seabed evolution during approximately one year following the extraction. These measurements are complemented by several sediment and ecological sampling campaigns and can be compared with a similar dataset acquired prior to sand extraction. Together, these observations provide a unique opportunity to analyze the physical processes controlling sediment transport and morphological adaptation following medium-depth sand extraction, while also providing the physical boundary conditions required to interpret ecological recolonization within the extraction pit. A major knowledge gap concerns how the interaction between waves, currents, turbulence, and evolving pit geometry controls sediment transport and ultimately determines the morphological recovery. In particular, it remains unclear whether recovery is dominated by rare high-energy storm events or by more progressive in filling, and how these processes vary through time as the pit evolves. Addressing these questions is essential for predicting the long-term impacts of offshore sand extraction and supporting nature-inclusive management of the North Sea.
The postdoctoral researcher will take the lead on the analysis of the hydrodynamic and morphodynamic measurements collected by the instrumented frames. An important part of the work will be to quantify how the sand extraction pit modifies waves, currents, and sediment transport processes, and to determine the mechanisms and timescales governing morphological recovery. Depending on the candidate's expertise and interests, the work may also include the application of numerical models to support the interpretation of field observations, or could include the development of innovative data-driven approaches to improve predictive capabilities. The postdoctoral researcher will furthermore closely interact with the ecological work package to establish the physical boundary conditions underpinning ecological recovery, with opportunities for a more active role in the integration of physical and ecological analyses if that fits the candidate’s interests.
The postdoctoral researcher will be based at TU Delft, working closely with Dr. Bram van Prooijen and Dr. Marion Tissier, while being embedded in a multidisciplinary research consortium involving several leading Dutch governmental, academic, and applied research institutions, including Rijkswaterstaat, Wageningen Marine Research, NIOZ, and Deltares.
This postdoctoral position is for a period of three years. The anticipated starting date is February/March 2027.
