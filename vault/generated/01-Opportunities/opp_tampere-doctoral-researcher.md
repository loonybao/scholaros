The Faculty of Social Sciences

The position is open within the discipline of psychology in the research project
PSYMORY – Understanding the Effects of Psychedelics on Autobiographical Memory
(PI Samuli Kangaslampi), funded by the Research Council of Finland. The project offers a unique opportunity to contribute to the emerging science of psychedelics in an inspiring, highly internationally connected working environment, with excellent opportunities for the researcher to develop their skills and career.
PSYMORY – Understanding the Effects of Psychedelics on Autobiographical Memory
seeks to uncover the effects of psychedelics on memory, especially whether they enhance or distort recall of past life events. It includes controlled human trials in collaboration with Maastricht University in the Netherlands, laboratory research at Tampere University with individuals taking psychedelics on their own in different contexts, and research based on surveys and existing reports on memory experiences under psychedelics. The findings are consolidated into a comprehensive model of how psychedelics affect autobiographical memory and guidelines for considering such effects in their therapeutic use and harm reduction. The project involves collaboration with Department of Neuropsychology and Psychopharmacology of Maastricht University and the Center for Psychedelic and Consciousness Research of Johns Hopkins University.
Position
We seek an outstanding doctoral researcher with a passion for advancing careful scientific research into the cognitive and subjective effects of psychedelics, ready to commit themselves to this endeavor in Tampere, Finland, for the next four years.
The doctoral researcher’s main duties will involve doctoral research on the research topic. The recruited researcher is expected to work closely with the PI and collaborators and contribute to realizing the joint research plan of the entire project. Part of this research will result in the doctoral researcher’s PhD thesis.
The doctoral researcher is expected to participate in regular project meetings, project planning and preparation, research design, data collection, handling, and analysis, preparing and publishing scientific articles, and other activities of the project. The appointed researcher will contribute to teaching and administrative tasks up to 5% of their working time. The working language of the PSYMORY project is English.
Qualifications
The selected candidate must hold a Master’s degree in a relevant field, such as psychology, psychopharmacology, or neuroscience. The selected candidate will need to apply for acceptance into Tampere University’s Doctoral Programme in Psychology and Logopedics during the Autumn semester of 2026.
The candidate should have the ability to conduct independent and collaborative research in psychology at a high international level. The candidate should have excellent analytical and methodological skills. Outstanding written and oral skills in English are required. Other language skills (e.g., Dutch, Spanish, Portuguese, Finnish, or non-European languages) are an advantage.
Previous research experience is a key advantage, especially experience in experimental research in cognitive neuroscience or cognitive psychology, research involving psychedelics or other psychoactive drugs, research involving virtual reality environments, psychometrics and survey design, and/or research involving complex study designs and sensitive research topics. Skills and experience in data management, advanced quantitative and qualitative research methods, the R programming language, and other programming skills are assets.
Demonstrated familiarity with the topics of psychedelic-assisted therapy, ethical and philosophical issues with psychedelics, psychotherapy, autobiographical memory, psychological trauma, and/or false memories will also be considered advantages.
Please be prepared to provide proof of your qualifications if requested. Please do not submit even partially AI-generated proposals.
