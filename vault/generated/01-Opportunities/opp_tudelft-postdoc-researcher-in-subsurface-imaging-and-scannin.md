## Description

Explore how electron signals can reveal hidden structures in next-generation semiconductor devices. Join TU Delft and ASML to advance scanning electron microscopy and develop innovative detection concepts that improve the imaging of structures below the surface.
Job description
Semiconductor devices are becoming increasingly small and complex. As a result, critical structures and defects are more difficult to detect using conventional inspection methods. Improving subsurface imaging is essential to advancing semiconductor technology and enabling the next generation of electronic devices.
At TU Delft, in collaboration with ASML, you will investigate how backscattered electrons (BSE) can reveal information about buried structures in semiconductor devices. Your research will focus on understanding which components of the electron signal contain the most useful information and how these can be detected more effectively.
You will explore four interconnected research themes: the physics and information content of backscattered electron signals, the influence of electron beam and sample parameters, the fundamental limits of resolution and detectability, and the development of detector concepts that capture more relevant information.
Combining theoretical modelling, scanning electron microscopy experiments and detector-concept development, you will translate fundamental insights into practical improvements in subsurface imaging. Your work will contribute to better resolution, improved signal-to-noise ratios and faster image acquisition.
You will work in an interdisciplinary research environment, collaborating with experts from TU Delft and ASML. You will also have opportunities to co-supervise PhD and MSc students, contribute to scientific discussions and strengthen your research leadership skills.
This postdoctoral position offers the opportunity to connect fundamental electron microscopy research with industrial challenges and contribute to innovations in semiconductor inspection. You will have room to develop your expertise, take ownership of research activities and build collaborations across academia and industry.
