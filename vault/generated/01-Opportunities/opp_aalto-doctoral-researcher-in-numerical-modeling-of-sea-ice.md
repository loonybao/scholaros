We are now looking for a
Doctoral Researcher in Numerical Modeling of Sea Ice
We are seeking a highly motivated Doctoral Researcher to join our ice mechanics research group at Aalto University to work on collaborative project between Aalto University and Finnish Meteorological Institute focusing on ice pile-ups and related risks to coastal structures and erosion. The ice rubble piles form as sea ice is pushed by wind towards the coastlines, fractures and fractured ice piles up.
Your role and goals
The successful candidate will conduct doctoral research on the numerical modelling of sea ice accumulations against coastline and coastal structures such as breakwaters by using computational tools developed in our research group. These tools are based on discrete element method and they have bee validated against experimental data. You will start your work by further developing the code to include time dependent cohesive bonding between the ice blocks. Then you will use the code to model ice pile-up. The work will have a multi-scale component as you will combine your detailed, engineering-scale modelling of ice pile-up formation with results from a large-scale sea ice model on regional scale ice conditions. Overall focus will be on identifying the critical combinations of ice and weather conditions that drive ice-related risks to coastal areas and structures. The modelling results will feed into the design of a warning and monitoring system concept for ice-induced risks, developed jointly with the Finnish Meteorological Institute. You will publish in leading scientific journals, present at conferences, participate in the research group's activities, and assist in advising master's students.
Your network and team
Your doctoral studies and research will be supervised by Associate Professor Arttu Polojärvi. You will work in his team and in close collaboration with the Finnish Meteorological Institute. You will also become part of the Marine and Arctic Technology research group at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, consisting of seven professors and a group of doctoral students and post-doctoral researchers.
Your experience and ambitions
We are looking for a candidate with a strong background in mechanics and computational engineering. Demonstrated interest in the physics of sea ice and computational modelling of ice will be considered as an advantage. Suitable backgrounds include solid mechanics, mechanical engineering, computational mechanics, civil engineering, applied physics, engineering mechanics, or naval architecture and marine engineering.
