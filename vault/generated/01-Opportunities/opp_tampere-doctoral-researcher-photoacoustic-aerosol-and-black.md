The Aerosol and Cloud Particle Optics group, part of the Aerosol Physics Laboratory at Tampere University, is establishing a new research direction in photoacoustic measurement technology within Tampere's strong aerosol research ecosystem. The group brings established expertise in photoacoustic and optical measurements, complemented by the Aerosol Physics Laboratory's long-standing expertise in aerosols and combustion processes. This work builds on monitoring-capable, multi-wavelength photoacoustic instrumentation, including PAAS-4λ, which has been operating at Hyytiälä since 2023.
Job description
As a doctoral researcher, you will carry out a focused research project on next-generation photoacoustic methods for aerosol and black-carbon measurements. Your core tasks will be to contribute to the development and construction of a new PAAS instrument version for laboratory testing and characterization, and to develop, test and validate new traceable calibration routines for photoacoustic and optical aerosol measurements. The work combines hands-on instrumentation, laboratory experiments, uncertainty analysis, programming and scientific data analysis.
A central motivation is Directive (EU) 2024/2881 on ambient air quality and cleaner air for Europe, which introduces black-carbon monitoring at urban and rural monitoring supersites. This creates a clear need for accurate, traceable and comparable optical measurements of equivalent black carbon (eBC).
As your expertise develops, you will have room to shape your own research questions. Possible directions include analysing existing data from sub-Arctic and Arctic regions, investigating how atmospheric processing changes the optical properties of black carbon, and developing ideas for future measurements.
Requirements
Applicants must hold an internationally recognized Master's degree in physics, engineering, aerosol or atmospheric science, optics, photonics, environmental science or another relevant field by the starting date. You should be motivated by experimental research, measurement technology and learning across disciplines.
The most important qualifications are hands-on experience with scientific instrumentation and strong programming and data-analysis skills in Python, MATLAB or comparable tools. Previous work with black-carbon or photoacoustic instruments is a significant advantage. Experience in aerosol measurements, optics, electronics, data acquisition, calibration or uncertainty analysis is also valuable. Excellent English and constructive teamwork are essential; Finnish is not required.
