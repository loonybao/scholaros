Project description
Third-cycle subject: Biological Physics
The Hannebelle Lab at the Division of Biophysics, Department of Applied Physics, studies the physical mechanisms of infection. We are based at KTH and SciLifeLab in Stockholm (
https://hannebellelab.org/
). We are seeking a PhD student to study the biophysics of schistosome skin infection. Schistosomiasis affects over 200 million people worldwide and is among the most prevalent neglected tropical diseases. Infection begins when schistosome parasites penetrate intact human skin from freshwater, a multi-hour process that remains largely uncharacterized in biophysical terms. Using in-vivo-mimetic systems, this project will investigate how these parasites find, penetrate, migrate through, and exit the skin, and what physical mechanisms govern each step. The work is highly interdisciplinary, bringing together microfluidics and organ-on-chip platforms, custom microscopy, cell culture, 3D printing and hardware design, and biophysical modeling. We do not expect applicants to arrive with expertise in all of these. What matters most is a demonstrated ability to learn new methods, a willingness to work across disciplines, and genuine curiosity about how physical principles shape biological processes. We are a young research group with a bold scientific vision, committed to supporting each member's growth into a skilled and independent researcher. You will join us at SciLifeLab in Solna.
