## Description

Job description
The Netherlands has a growing need for temporary sustainable electricity, driven by grid congestion, emission-free construction, and energy independence, including in defence. Today, diesel generators mostly fill this role, with the associated CO₂, NOx, and particulate emissions, fuel logistics, and licensing hurdles. Mobile wind energy, combined with PV, batteries, and backup, can offer a sustainable alternative.
For mobile systems in the 50–200 kW class, transportability is a key barrier: rotors of approximately 20–35 m diameter do not fit in standard 20 ft containers. Modular segmented blades could solve this, but they introduce uncertainties in aerodynamics, aeroelastic behaviour, structural integrity, fatigue, acoustics, and safety.
In this industry-academia project, TU Delft works with TouchWind (system integrator and project coordinator) and eProWind (module interfaces, composite structures, and fatigue) to develop and validate a modular rotor concept. The project provides the R&D basis for a first mobile 50 kW application.
Your role
You will lead TU Delft’s contribution to the experimental validation and modelling of aerodynamic and aeroelastic effects of segmented rotor blades. You will work closely with industrial partners.
Responsibilities
Design, perform, and analyse wind tunnel experiments on segmented rotor blades and rotor models, including the effects of segment joints and interfaces on flow behaviour and performance.
Quantify the impact of blade segmentation on aerodynamic efficiency, energy yield, loads, and acoustics.
Interpret aerodynamic and aeroelastic effects, combining measurements with engineering models and, where useful, higher-fidelity simulations.
Support the validation of aeroelastic models developed by TouchWind, including load cases and load transfer through the modular connections.
Contribute to the assessment of structural integrity and fatigue aspects together with the project partners.
Coordinate experimental campaigns, data management, communication, and reporting with industrial partners.
Publish results in peer-reviewed journals and conferences, and contribute to knowledge transfer towards industry.
Co-supervise MSc and support PhD students working on related topics.
Job requirements
PhD in aerospace, mechanical, or wind energy engineering, or a closely related field.
Strong background in rotor aerodynamics and/or aeroelasticity.
Hands-on experience with experimental work, preferably wind tunnel testing, including measurement techniques such as load, pressure, PIV, or strain measurements.
Experience with aeroelastic or aerodynamic modelling (e.g. BEM, lifting-line, or CFD) and with scientific programming (Python or MATLAB).
A track record of scientific publications.
Ability to work independently and in a multidisciplinary team, and comfortable collaborating with industry partners.
Excellent communication skills in English.
