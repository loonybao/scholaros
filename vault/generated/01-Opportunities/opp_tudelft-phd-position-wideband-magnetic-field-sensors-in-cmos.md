## Description

Develop next-generation CMOS magnetic sensors at TU Delft. Combine sensor physics and analog circuit design to advance wideband magnetic sensing technology.
Job description
At TU Delft, we conduct pioneering research into sensor technology and integrated circuit design. As a PhD researcher in wideband magnetic field sensors in CMOS, you will contribute to the development of next-generation magnetic sensors, with a particular focus on the Hall effect in silicon.
You will combine your knowledge of sensor physics and analog circuit design to investigate, design and evaluate innovative sensor solutions. Through experimental work and simulations, you will explore new approaches to improving sensor performance and bandwidth. Your work will contribute to advancing CMOS-based magnetic sensing technology and its applications.
You will join the group of Professor Kofi Makinwa, where you will have the opportunity to contribute your expertise to cutting-edge research in analog circuit design and sensor systems. You will independently conduct research, analyse results and communicate your findings to the scientific community through publications and presentations.
Job requirements
You hold an MSc degree in Electrical Engineering or a closely related field.
You have a solid understanding of sensor physics, particularly about Hall sensors.
You have a strong background in analog circuit design and sensor-based systems.
You have demonstrable experience in integrated circuit (IC) design, evidenced by at least one publication in an IEEE journal on circuit design, such as the IEEE Journal of Solid-State Circuits (JSSC).
You can independently conduct experimental and simulation-based research.
You communicate scientific results clearly in writing and presentations.
You have an excellent command of written and spoken English.
