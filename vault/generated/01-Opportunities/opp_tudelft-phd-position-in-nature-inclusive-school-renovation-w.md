## Description

Interested in biobased materials and sustainable renovation? This PhD investigates nature-inclusive renovation design for climate resilience and healthier learning environments.
Job description
The Faculty of Architecture and the Built Environment in Delft University of technology is looking for a PhD researcher to work on a NWO-KIC Nature-based Solutions research project entitled "School Greenovation", supervised by Associate Professor Thaleia Konstantinou and Assistant Professor Olga Ioannou.
The project aims to strengthen climate resilience in schools, reduce CO
₂
emissions and lower long-term operational costs while ensuring healthier learning environments. It adopts a whole-systems approach, where renovation decisions for school buildings are informed by the dynamic relationships between materials, biodiversity, and child development. The research prioritizes long-term resilience over short-term efficiency. It considers school buildings as agents of dynamic local ecosystems that a. integrate flora, fauna, and microbiomes, b. support biodiversity and ecological balance, and c. foster meaningful interactions between children and nature that enhance their connection to the environment and promote environmental stewardship.
The aim of this PhD research is to provide detailed technical and ecological insights to support designers, school boards and associations, construction companies and policymakers to select and implement nature-inclusive, climate-resilient refurbishment strategies in schools. On the one hand, it focuses on increasing the use of circular and biobased materials based on availability, functionality, cost, ecological value, and educational affordances. On the other hand, it opts to develop the necessary knowledge about how these materials can be integrated in renovations of schools, and which functionalities they can support in building, ecology and education.
Key research activities include:
mapping state-of-the-art nature-inclusive, climate-resilient approaches and surveying the current state of the demonstrators to assess energy systems/efficiency, vulnerabilities/construction quality, and the physical characteristics (materiality, design, building envelope performance).
developing regenerative, nature-inclusive refurbishment strategies for school buildings together with local key stakeholders.
translating strategies to clear guidelines for renovations of sustainable school buildings inspired by nature and tailoring the guidelines for each demonstrator.
crafting scaled models and 1:1 scaled prototypes (one per demonstrator).
contributing to the writing of high-quality, high-impact publications and present findings at international conferences.
You will be part of a transdisciplinary consortium that consists of 15+ members. You will be involved in real-life experimentation in three school buildings in the NL.
This PhD position is for four years and will lead to a doctoral degree from TU Delft. The candidate will publish scientific articles, participate in international workshops and consortium meetings, and contribute modestly to teaching activities within the faculty.
