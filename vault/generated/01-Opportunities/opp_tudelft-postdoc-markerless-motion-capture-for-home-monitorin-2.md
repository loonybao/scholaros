Job description
The department of Biomechanical Engineering TU Delft seeks a motivated postdoctoral researcher with a background in biomechanical engineering for a full-time, fully funded project on the clinical validation of single-camera markerless motion capture for assessment and long-term home monitoring of motor impairments in stroke and Parkinson's disease patients. The position is based at TU Delft, supervised by Dr. Eline van der Kruk and Dr. Ajay Seth, with measurements at clinical partner sites and in patients' homes, and part of your time spent on-site at Model Health (Leuven, Belgium) to co-develop the home-based prototype. You will work closely with clinicians, patients, and Model Health, embedded in the multidisciplinary "Care is coming home!" program, a 5-year consortium of 2 technical universities (TU Delft, UTwente), 6 hospitals, 13 companies (including Model Health), 1 rehabilitation centre, and 5 patient organisations.
The Challenge
Reliable, long-term monitoring of motor impairments outside the clinic is essential to support home-based rehabilitation, yet existing markerless motion capture systems are rarely validated on neurological gait patterns and struggle to generalize across pathology, body morphology, camera viewpoint, and home environments. Before such systems can support clinical decisions, we need to know how accurate they really are, for which patients, and under which conditions. We also need to understand which outcome measures are valid and meaningful to patients, caregivers and clinicians.This position aims to close that gap: you will verify and validate a single-camera markerless motion capture system against gold-standard 3D marker-based motion capture and musculoskeletal models, first in the lab and then in patients' homes, linking biomechanical accuracy to clinically meaningful outcomes.
You will:
• Assess the accuracy of markerless outcome measures against marker-based 3D motion capture and musculoskeletal modelling in people with stroke and Parkinson’s disease.
• Use musculoskeletal modelling to compare markerless and marker-based estimates, evaluate their biomechanical plausibility and perform sensitivity analyses
• Evaluate whether these outcome measures are valid, meaningful, and acceptable in the home environment, in collaboration with patients, caregivers and clinicians
• Generate biomechanically realistic synthetic movement data from musculoskeletal simulations to train and improve markerless motion capture models
• Design and carry out home measurements with a single-camera prototype developed together with Model Health, and compare its real-world performance with lab-based measurements
• Contribute to clinician-facing visualization of the results, with privacy-by-design protection of patients and their home environment
You will translate this into a research plan aimed at peer-reviewed publications, alongside consortium deliverables.
