Project description
Third-cycle subject:
Sustainability studies
We are seeking applicants with an interest in wetland restoration as a nature-based solution for sustainable water supply under changing climate conditions. The  will combine methods such as policy analysis, value mapping, socio-economic analysis, and qualitative research methods. The overall aim is to develop policy and valuation frameworks that link wetland indicators to societal benefits and costs, as well as to practical policy instruments and financing mechanisms. The successful applicant will primarily contribute to: (1) developing policy-relevant indicators for the water-related benefits of restored wetlands and exploring how these can be translated into information relevant for valuation, decision-making, and policy development; and (2) analyzing and mapping the values generated by wetland restoration for different stakeholders and society as a whole, including benefits, costs, and risks, as well as how these are distributed among different actors.
The work will be carried out in close collaboration with researchers and societal stakeholders in two interdisciplinary research projects. The position is based at the Department of Sustainable Development, Environmental Science and Engineering (SEED).
