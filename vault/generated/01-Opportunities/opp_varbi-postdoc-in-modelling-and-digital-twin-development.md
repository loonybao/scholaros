Job description
Would you like to build a physics-based model and a digital twin of how bio-based materials are pressed and dried? We are looking for a postdoc at the Department of Fibre and Polymer Technology at KTH and the Wallenberg Wood Science Center.
In a Formas-funded project together with Hitachi Energy and Nordic Seafarm, we develop knowledge and tools to reduce the energy used in the dewatering and drying of bio-based materials. Your role is to develop a robust numerical model of mechanical dewatering and subsequent thermal drying and to advance it into a validated digital twin of the process.
You will work within a continuum porous-media framework – Darcy flow coupled to heat transfer with phase change – and implement the model in existing codes and/or COMSOL, including AI-tools. You will calibrate and validate the model against experimental data, demonstrate a digital twin using industrial or lab data, and work closely with an experimental postdoc and industrial partners.
Qualifications
Requirements
A doctoral degree or an equivalent foreign degree. This eligibility requirement must be met no later than the time the employment decision is made.
Documented scientific expertise in numerical modelling and simulation of coupled physical phenomena, demonstrated through publications.
Experience of finite element or finite volume methods and scientific programming, e.g. Python, MATLAB, C++ or Fortran.
Very good command of English, spoken and written, as it is required in the daily work.
As a person you are independent, take initiative and have a strong ability to collaborate in an interdisciplinary environment.

A doctoral degree or an equivalent foreign degree, obtained within the last three years prior to the application deadline
Experience of COMSOL Multiphysics or equivalent multiphysics simulation software.
Experience of modelling dewatering, drying or wet pressing of pulp, paper or fibre networks.
Experience of calibrating and validating models against experimental data, or of digital twins and reduced-order models.
Teaching abilities.
