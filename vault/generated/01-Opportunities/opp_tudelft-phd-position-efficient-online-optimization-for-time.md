## Description

How to avoid learning too much - safe and efficient learning for control
Job description
Inefficient operation of industrial systems, electricity production, and heating systems in buildings is currently responsible for over 50% of overall greenhouse gases emissions. Improving energy efficiency of energy-intensive sectors by implementing advanced control systems has the potential to both improve efficiency and reduce the costs, all while continuing to meet safety requirements. The flexibility of safe and adaptive learning control methods makes them crucial for safety-critical applications. A key difficulty in these learning-based approaches lies in the fact that the algorithm must simultaneously learn the model and guarantee safety. This dual requirement significantly impacts the computational cost of the learning algorithm, particularly in situations where the optimal decision evolves over time. We now aim to develop computationally efficient safe learning methods for time-varying optimal control problems.
Existing safe learning algorithms usually assume that they have unlimited access to the experiments and thus unlimited new data. Approaches using limited data sets have been restricted to Bayesian optimization without safety constraints. Moreover, the algorithms typically rely on the repetitive nature of the experiments, for instance in robotics or precision manufacturing. The unlimited data combined with repetitiveness limit the applicability of safe learning algorithms in problems evolving over time, such as residential buildings. This PhD project will explore the connection between the dynamics of the optimized system with numerical properties of optimization algorithms to provide a framework for safe learning with minimal interactions over time. Sitting at the intersection of theory and applications, the project includes experimental validation of the developed algorithms in collaboration with Dr Giulia de Pasquale from TU Eindhoven (laboratory tests) and partners in Switzerland (Next Evolution in Sustainable Building Technologies (NEST) residential building).
The PhD would join the group of Dr Marta Zagorowska at Delft Center for Systems and Control (DCSC).
