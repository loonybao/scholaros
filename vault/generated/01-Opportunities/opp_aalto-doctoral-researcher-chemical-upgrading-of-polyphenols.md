About the Bioproduct Chemistry group
The Bioproduct Chemistry group, lead by Professor Monika Österberg, aims to replace fossil‑based materials with renewable biomaterials from natural resources, especially forest biomass. We specialize in bio‑based materials science and the surface chemistry of lignocellulosic materials, with emphasis on fundamental insights and interaction forces.
Our research themes include:
Renewable and recyclable materials from cellulose, lignin, and hemicelluloses
Valorization of polyphenolic side streams and greener processes (e.g., mechanochemistry, surface modification)
Interfacial phenomena and development of biobased barriers, foams, and emulsions
Advanced textile recycling, including flotation‑based methods

Position description
Are you excited about sustainability and renewable materials? Bark and lignin are abundant, underutilized feedstocks rich in polyphenols. When engineered to self‑assemble into spherical colloidal particles, these compounds can replace fossil‑based or harmful chemicals across coatings, packaging, emulsifiers, and other applications.
In this PhD project, you will work at crossroads of natural products chemistry, material science, and industry, specifically:
Develop efficient fractionation and modification routes for polyphenols, including solvent‑free reactive extrusion
Establish structure–property–performance relationships linking chemical composition and particle size to application performance
Combine wet‑chemistry characterization (e.g., 1H/13C/ 2D NMR, AFM, DLS, GPC, FTIR, zeta potential) with molecular modeling and machine learning
Collaborate with international academic and industrial partners
Who we’re looking for
