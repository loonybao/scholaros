## Description

Future robots must perceive, predict, communicate, and decide reliably in complex, human–robot shared environments. The Mobile Robotics (
https://www.aalto.fi/en/department-of-electrical-engineering-and-automation/mobile-robotics
) and Intelligent Robotics (
https://irobotics.aalto.fi
) groups at Aalto University’s School of Electrical Engineering develop autonomy under imperfect knowledge and uncertain sensing, with emphasis on safe operation among people, socially aware perception and behaviour prediction, and semantic/dynamic environment understanding. Our research environment offers close collaboration with leading academic and industrial partners, access to state-of-the-art robot platforms, full-scale industrial sites, multi-sensor labs, and advanced network testbeds. The teams are highly international, and the working language is English.
We are seeking excellent candidates for
doctoral researcher
positions to participate in four recently awarded new research endeavours:
Mobile Robotics - Safe Autonomy in Shared Industrial Environments
We are seeking an excellent candidate at the doctoral researcher level to develop perception, prediction, and planning methods that enable autonomous machines to operate safely in human-robot shared industrial spaces. The research will model how people and machines move through dynamic environments to support human detection and tracking, spatio-temporal mapping, long-horizon human-motion prediction, and planning under uncertainty. The methodological scope includes probabilistic modeling, SLAM/localization, spatio-temporal representations, and decision-making under uncertainty. The work is part of Autonomy without Fences (AwF, 2026–2029) and will be validated at real sites (ports, underground mines, logistics terminals) with Kalmar, Normet, Sandvik, and KWH Logistics.
Supervisor:
Assistant Professor Tomasz Kucner (
tomasz.kucner@aalto.fi
)
Semantic Communication for Mission-Critical Robotics
We are seeking an excellent candidate at the doctoral researcher level to co-design task-aware perception and NextG wireless networking so robots exchange meaning rather than raw data. The research will determine what information a robot needs for coordination and feed this into network design. The methodological scope includes multi-robot coordination, task-aware perception and representation, semantic and goal-oriented communication, and NextG mechanisms (e.g., slicing, time-sensitive networking). The work is funded by a Finland-US joint project in partnership with MIT, Stevens Institute, Nokia Bell Labs, and pilots with leading Finnish companies.
Supervisor:
Assistant Professor Tomasz Kucner (
tomasz.kucner@aalto.fi
)
Distributed Situational Awareness and Semantic Scene Understanding
We are seeking an excellent candidate at the doctoral researcher level to co-design edge and centralized intelligence for mission-critical situational awareness in dynamic environments. You will build and maintain real-time semantic 3D scene representations across heterogeneous sensors and robots, deciding what to infer locally and what to aggregate centrally, how to communicate semantics efficiently, and how to update, query, and predict scene state as conditions change. The research will focus on multimodal perception (vision–language models), probabilistic state estimation and 3D mapping/SLAM, and graph-based representations (scene/knowledge graphs). The work is funded by a Finland-US joint project in partnership with MIT, Stevens Institute, Nokia Bell Labs, and pilots with leading Finnish companies.
Supervisors:
Dr. Francesco Verdoja (
francesco.verdoja@aalto.fi
) and Prof. Ville Kyrki (
ville.kyrki@aalto.fi
)
Socially Aware Perception and Behavior Prediction
We are seeking an excellent candidate at the doctoral researcher level to develop socially aware perception and behavior-prediction methods for autonomous systems operating in dynamic environments shared with humans. The research will investigate how autonomous systems can combine sensory information with contextual and behavioral cues to understand human actions, anticipate future behavior, and reason under uncertainty. The methodological scope includes machine learning, computer vision, multimodal perception, probabilistic modeling, and interpretable artificial intelligence. The research will be conducted in collaboration with leading national and international academic partners and validated using real-world and simulated autonomous-system scenarios.
Supervisors:
Dr. Farzeen Munir (PI,
farzeen.munir@aalto.fi
) and Prof. Ville Kyrki (
ville.kyrki@aalto.fi
)
