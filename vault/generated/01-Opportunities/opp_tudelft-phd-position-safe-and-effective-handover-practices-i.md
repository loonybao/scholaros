## Description

Safe and effective handover practices in sociotechnical Intensive Care Unit context
Job description
The Intensive Care Unit (ICU) is a highly complex and dynamic environment where critically ill patients require continuous monitoring and multidisciplinary care. Safe transfer of information during handovers between healthcare professionals is essential for maintaining continuity of care, reducing errors, and ensuring patient safety. ICU handovers occur frequently, including shift changes, transfers between units, and transitions of care involving physicians, nurses, and other healthcare professionals.
Despite increasing attention to patient safety, substantial variation exists in how handovers are performed across hospitals and clinical teams. Structured handover tools, electronic support systems, and standardized communication frameworks (e.g., SBAR, I-PASS) have been introduced to improve information transfer. However, the implementation and effectiveness of these interventions vary considerably, and handovers remain vulnerable to communication failures, interruptions, information loss, cognitive overload and sociotechnical challenges.
This PhD project aims to examine ICU handover practices in their sociotechnical context, how they are implemented and adapted in clinical settings, their impact on patient safety and care outcomes, and the system factors that facilitate or hinder effective communication during transitions of care.
