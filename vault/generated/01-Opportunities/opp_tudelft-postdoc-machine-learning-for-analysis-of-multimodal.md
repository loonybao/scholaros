## Description

Build models to connect catalytic data and unravel new chemsitries. Join TU Delft and HyFINE to work bridge catalysis fundamentals and application with machine learning.
Job description
We are seeking a motivated and experienced postdoctoral researcher to develop machine-learning approaches for integrating diverse catalytic datasets. The aim is to connect catalyst identity and its evolution under reaction conditions with activity, selectivity and lifetime, and to provide practical guidance to experimental and comptuational colleagues in their search for better understanding of the catalytic phenomena and design principles.
This position is part of the HyFINE collaborative program developing new chemical technologies based on the use of green hydrogen and electrons for sustainable production of fine and speciality chemicals.
You will join the group of prof. Evgeny Pidko of the Functional Molecues and Materials section in the Department of Chemical Engineering at TU Delft, and collaborate with experimental and computational chemists in the group as well as various academic and industrial HyFINE partners.
Working closely with experimental and computational colleagues, you will develop approaches for integrating and analyzing complementary information from activity screenings, time-resolved reactivity and spectroscopy experiments as well as computational studies on transition metal-based homogeneous catalyst systems. You will address differences in data structure, coverage, experimental protocols and uncertainty, while preserving the chemical context and provenance of the given findings and observations.
In this position you will:
Develop reproducible workflows for curating and integrating heterogeneous catalytic data from different sources and research communities.
Design and evaluate representations connecting molecular structure, catalyst speciation, reaction conditions and measured catalytic behaviour.
Develop and benchmark statistical and machine-learning approaches for complementary, incomplete or unevenly sampled datasets.
Assess generalisation across catalysts, substrates, conditions and data sources using appropriate baselines, validation strategies and uncertainty analysis.
Work with experimental researchers to turn model results into testable chemical hypotheses and identify informative new experiments.
Deliver documented, reusable analysis workflows and communicate results through publications, consortium meetings and student supervision.
We offer
An interdisciplinary environment connecting machine learning with molecular chemistry, kinetics, spectroscopy and computation; collaboration with researchers generating catalytic datasets; and opportunities to develop methods that expand accessible chemical space and explain catalyst behaviour.
The starting date will be agreed with the successful candidate.
