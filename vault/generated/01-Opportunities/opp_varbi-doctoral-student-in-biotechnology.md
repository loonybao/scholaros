Project description
Third-cycle subject:
Biotechnology
At the Department of Protein Technology, we develop predictable and robust technologies for protein production, purification, and detection using small, structurally well-characterized protein domains as building blocks for new molecular functions. This PhD project builds on our recently developed calcium-regulated affinity binders (CaRAs), which bind their targets in the presence of calcium and release them at low calcium concentrations. By combining protein and protein engineering with biophysical characterization, structural biology, computational modelling, and machine learning, you will investigate how amino acid sequence and environmental conditions govern this behaviour. The project brings together the expertise built in Professor Sophia Hober´s group at KTH Royal Institute of Technology and Professor Andreas Hougaard Laustsen’s gruop at the Technical University of Denmark. The project aims to uncover the principles governing environmentally regulated protein function and translate them into design rules for a new generation of smarter and more precise therapeutic proteins.
