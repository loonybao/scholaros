## Description

Do you want to understand how to organise the transition to circular consumption of e-bikes, employing stakeholder workshops, scenarios, and pathways, and diving into circular consumption ecosystems?
Job description
If this question sparks your interest and you are motivated to pursue a PhD in circular consumption niches and the transition to circular consumption, you might be our new colleague for the Circular Consumption through Value-Chain Integration and Co-Creation for the Circular Economy (CIVIC-4CE) project. We are seeking an excellent candidate for a 4-year PhD study as part of the CIVIC-4CE project, an interdisciplinary and transdisciplinary research project funded by NWO, the Netherlands Research Council, under the call on KIC Consumer behaviour for a circular system transition. See:
https://www.tudelft.nl/en/2026/tbm/tpm-researchers-receive-nwo-kic-consumer-behaviour-for-a-circular-system-transition-grant
.
The project includes 2 PhD positions, 2 Postdoc positions, and several researchers at Universities of Applied Sciences. CIVIC-4CE studies how changing consumer behaviour can accelerate the Dutch transition to a circular society. The project focuses on how changing circular consumption contributes to extending product lifetime, retaining material value, and reducing waste, with cases on e-bikes and electronics (washing machines). Using a transdisciplinary research approach that includes stakeholder engagement, behavioural science, design research, transition studies, and modelling, the project aims to develop empirically grounded interventions and governance strategies that enable lasting behavioural change towards circular consumption.
Within this PhD vacancy, you will work in the work package that focuses on systemic transitions towards circular consumption and production through integrating behavioural change, visions and transition pathways, modelling, and policy mixes. The work combines qualitative and quantitative methods, including stakeholder workshops, interviews, backcasting, transition pathways, policy mixes, social simulation modelling, and possibly participatory value evaluation or gamification. You will collaborate with other early-career and senior researchers in the project, as well as other consortium partners. You will also reach out to relevant stakeholders, innovating companies, and policymakers to gather data and validate results. Your main supervisors will be Assistant Professor Jaco Quist and Associate Professor Emile Chappin.
