Project description
Third-cycle subject: Engineering Mechanics
FLOW
is a strong community of researchers engaged in fundamental and applied research in fluid mechanics within the department of Engineering Mechanics.
We are currently seeking an experimental PhD student in fluid mechanics to join the Fluids and Surfaces Group within FLOW. This position offers an exciting opportunity for a highly motivated candidate to contribute to a cutting-edge research program funded by the Swedish Research Council (VR).
The project aims to uncover how complex films, including biological and living films, interact with surrounding fluid flows. Such films are ubiquitous in nature and technological applications, ranging from mucus in the lungs to biofilms on medical devices and ship hulls. When exposed to shear and potentially unsteady flows, these films can deform into complex shapes, exhibit unusual material properties, and evolve over time, thereby creating a dynamic and continuously changing boundary for the flow. At the same time, the films modify the overlying flow, potentially altering frictional resistance as well as the transport and exchange of species.
Despite their importance, the coupled interaction between complex films and fluid flows remains poorly understood. This limits our ability to predict and control these systems and poses major challenges across medical, industrial, and geophysical applications.
In this project, the PhD student will work with and further develop a unique experimental platform for studying complex films under controlled, yet complex, flow conditions. The experimental work will be complemented by continuum modeling of complex materials, data-driven methods, and computational fluid dynamics.
