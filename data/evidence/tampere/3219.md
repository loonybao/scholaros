Job description
We are looking for one (1) Doctoral Researcher in the areas of vehicle dynamics, automatic control and mechatronics. Successful candidates will pursue a doctoral degree at Tampere University. Full-time doctoral studies are expected to be completed in three years.
You will focus on the research of experimental methods on onboard railway wheel friction estimate and control. You will collaborate closely with one doctoral researcher at Machine Dynamic Group who develops computational methods for model-based rail friction estimation. During your PhD you will have opportunity to test your technology on full-scale wheel-rail lab (
Full-Scale Wheel-Rail Lab | Tampere University
) at Tampere University and real train operated in Finland. You will have the opportunity for the research exchange in other EU countries. You will work for the project funded by Research Council of Finland (
RAILCLIMA | Tampere Universities
).
Your work will include:
Develop and validate experimental methods and advanced sensor instrumentation for onboard wheel–rail friction estimation.
Investigate the operational and environmental conditions under which wheel–rail friction can be estimated accurately and reliably.
Develop methods for wheel slip detection using existing vehicle sensor signals and operational data.
Design and implement hardware-in-the-loop (HIL) simulation platforms for wheel friction control.
As a Doctoral Researcher you are expected to perform overviews of state of the art, to study the principles of vehicle dynamics, friction formulation, mechatronics, signal processing and vibration analysis and experimentation instrumentation. You are also expected to develop the computational algorithms, to generate experimental results, to analyze and validate them on use case(s) through comparison with the state of the art.
The results of your research will be disseminated in scientific peer-reviewed journals, international conferences, doctoral network events and events with industry. The tasks of doctoral researchers also include teaching duties that amount to approximately 5% of their annual working hours, unless there are justified reasons to the contrary.
