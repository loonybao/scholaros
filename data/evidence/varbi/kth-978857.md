PhD student in Agentic & Embodied AI for Adaptive Manufacturing Systems
Apply for position
KTH, School of Industrial Engineering and Management
Project description
Third-cycle subject:
Production engineering
We are seeking a motivated PhD student to develop AI and robotic methods for adaptive manufacturing systems, with a particular focus on agentic and embodied AI. The research addresses how robots and intelligent production systems can perceive changing conditions, reason about process requirements and constraints, and adapt their actions in real time. The project brings together task level decision intelligence, multimodal perception, digital twin based learning and robot control to enable more flexible and intelligent manufacturing.
The research will use battery assembly and disassembly as application scenarios.
The PhD student will develop and experimentally validate AI enabled robotic methods in these scenarios, while addressing research questions that are relevant to a broader range of adaptive manufacturing systems.
The position is part of a joint PhD program between KTH and Shanghai Jiao Tong University (SJTU). The PhD student will be full time employed at KTH and spend at least 12 months at SJTU, distributed across several research stays. Upon successful completion of the joint PhD programme, KTH and SJTU will jointly issue the PhD degree, with reference to the national accreditation authorized for each university.

Information regarding admission and employment
Only those admitted to postgraduate education may be employed as a doctoral student. The total length of employment may not be longer than what corresponds to full-time doctoral education in four years' time. An employed doctoral student can, to a limited extent (maximum 20%), perform certain tasks within their role, e.g. training and administration. A new position as a doctoral student is for a maximum of one year, and then the employment may be renewed for a maximum of two years at a time. In the case of studies that are to be completed with a licentiate degree, the total period of employment may not be longer than what corresponds to full-time doctoral education for two years.
