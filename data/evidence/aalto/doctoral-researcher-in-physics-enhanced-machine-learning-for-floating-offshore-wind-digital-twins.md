The School of Engineering is inviting applications for a Doctoral Researcher in Physics-Enhanced Machine Learning for Floating Offshore Wind Digital Twins
Floating offshore wind turbines will be central to the clean-energy transition, but their behaviour at sea is difficult to predict and monitor. They move in six degrees of freedom under irregular waves and turbulent wind. The mooring lines that keep them in position are located deep underwater, where permanent sensing is costly and difficult to maintain. Operators therefore need digital twins that combine hydrodynamic knowledge with sensor data to estimate quantities that cannot be measured directly.
In this full-time, salaried Doctoral Researcher position, you will help develop the next generation of these digital twins: models that capture the wave-driven dynamics of floating turbines, update as new data arrive, detect early signs of degradation, and quantify the uncertainty of their estimates.
The position is funded by the Research Council of Finland. It is based at Aalto University's Otaniemi campus in Espoo, Finland, and starts on 28 January 2027or as agreed.
Research environment and duties
At the
Department of Energy and Mechanical Engineering,
our Marine and Arctic Technology research group studies how ships and offshore structures move and are loaded in waves, wind and ice. We combine machine learning and data-driven methods with hydrodynamic theory, numerical simulation and model-scale experiments. In this role, you will help us build digital twins that track the wave-induced motions, mooring loads and structural condition of floating offshore wind turbines in real time using only a small number of sensors.
Your work will contribute to safer and more cost-effective floating wind farms, where wave loads and mooring tensions can be monitored continuously, degradation can be detected early, and maintenance can be planned before failures occur.
Your work will include:
Developing physics-enhanced machine-learning models that learn the coupled hydrodynamic and structural dynamics of floating wind turbines from measured data and update in real time. These methods will build on, among other approaches, Koopman operator theory and dynamic mode decomposition (DMD).
Embedding hydrodynamic knowledge into these models so that they remain physically consistent across sea states, including wave excitation, radiation-memory effects, slow-drift motions and mooring-line dynamics.
Estimating wave-induced motions and mooring loads that cannot be measured directly.
Detecting early mooring-system or structural degradation and distinguishing it from normal changes in waves, wind and turbine operating conditions.
Quantifying the uncertainty of these estimates using Bayesian filtering and probabilistic machine learning.
Generating and analysing data from: hydrodynamic simulations, including potential-flow methods and, where needed, computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and coupled simulation tools such as OpenFAST
Publishing results in leading international journals and presenting them at conferences.
You will work closely with researchers in the Marine and Arctic Technology group and with our international academic collaborators.
This is an exciting time to join us. Floating offshore wind is scaling up rapidly, and industry guidance increasingly favours indirect monitoring of hard-to-access components, such as mooring lines, using sensors located elsewhere on the structure.
Our group has recently shown that digital twins learned directly from measurements can estimate unmeasured responses and loads from only a few sensors, including verification using wave-basin test data. In this role, you will take these methods a step further by embedding hydrodynamics into the learning process and moving from accurate estimation toward reliable, self-updating condition monitoring.
What you bring
To thrive in this role, you will need:
A Master's degree completed by the start of the position. Suitable backgrounds include naval architecture, marine or ocean engineering, offshore engineering, mechanical, civil or aerospace engineering, applied mathematics, physics, computer science, or a related field.
A strong foundation in linear algebra and in at least one of the following areas: hydrodynamics or fluid mechanics, structural dynamics, dynamical systems, or signal processing.
Good programming skills in Python or a similar language, together with a genuine interest in machine learning.
Curiosity, independence, and the ability to communicate clearly and work well in a team.
We also value:
Knowledge of marine or offshore hydrodynamics, such as wave-structure interaction, potential-flow or boundary-element methods, or computational fluid dynamics (e.g. OpenFOAM or REEF3D).
Experience with OpenFAST, MoorDyn or WAMIT, or similar software.
Experience with dynamic mode decomposition, Koopman operator methods, system identification or operational modal analysis.
Experience with Kalman filtering, Bayesian inference, Gaussian processes or uncertainty quantification.
Experience with deep learning for time series, such as Transformers or graph neural networks, using PyTorch or JAX.
Scientific publications or a strong Master's thesis in a related area.
