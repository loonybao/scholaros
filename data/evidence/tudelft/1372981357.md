Challenge: A Readiness Assessment Methodology for quantum technologies
Change: Adjust the UNESCO RAM to quantum technologies
Impact: Allow a global understanding of the impact of quantum technologies
Job description
Quantum technologies are rapidly evolving from prototypes in labs to computers, networks and sensors that can be deployed in the world. The functionality of these technologies is already articulated to some extent, but applications relevant for people, industry and governments are less well defined or understood. This lack of understanding holds for countries that are developing quantum technologies and even more so for countries that do not share in this development.
In order to better understand what quantum technologies may mean for countries of the second category, the candidate will engage in the following activities:
Study methodology by which UNESCO explores the impact of technologies on communities globally
Transpose this methodology to one that can explore the societal and cultural impact of quantum technologies
Test the transposition with communities in Africa or Latin America
Tasks and responsibilities:
Formulate and test the UNESCO readiness assessment methodology
You will be part of the Ethics/Philosophy of Technology section at the Faculty of Technology, Policy and Management, and work within the Dutch QDNL programme. You work together with Dr Pieter Vermaas, TU Delft, and collaborate with researchers working on ethical, legal and society issues part of Action Line 4 of
QDNL
Partake in the teaching of Ethics/Philosophy of Technology section
